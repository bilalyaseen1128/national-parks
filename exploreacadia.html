A Brief History

The history of Acadia National Park dates back to the early 20th century, when private citizens recognized the need to protect the beautiful landscapes of Mount Desert Island in Maine. In 1916, President Woodrow Wilson established the Sieur de Monts National Monument, marking the beginning of federal protection for the area. This made Acadia one of the first national parks established east of the Mississippi River.

Finally, in 1929, the park received its permanent name, Acadia National Park, to honor the early French settlers of the region, known as “Acadians.” Over the years, it has become a symbol of conservation and natural beauty, attracting millions of visitors annually. The park preserves a unique blend of mountains, forests, lakes, and rocky shores, showcasing the timeless charm and history of New England’s coastal wilderness.
