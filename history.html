DISCOVER THE HISTORY

Yellowstone National Park

Yellowstone's history began over 11,000 years ago with Indigenous peoples, followed by European American explorers and the establishment of the world's first national park on March 1, 1872. The park was created to preserve its unique geothermal features and wildlife for public enjoyment, with President Ulysses S. Grant signing the Yellowstone National Park Protection Act. Key events include the Hayden Expedition of 1871, which helped lead to the park's creation, and the later arrival of the U.S. Army to protect it from poaching.

Yosemite National Park

The history of Yosemite National Park dates back thousands of years, beginn ing with the Indigenous Ahwahneechee people who called the valley home. They lived in deep connection with the land, relying on its rivers, forests, and meadows for survival. In 1851, European American explorers arrived, leading to increased interest in the area’s breathtaking landscapes. Recognizing its beauty and importance, President Abraham Lincoln signed the Yosemite Grant in 1864 , protecting the land for public use — a first in American history. Later, the passionate work of naturalist John Muir led to Yosemite becoming an official national park in 1890, marking the start of the U.S. national park movement.

Acadia National Park

The history of Acadia National Park began in the early 1900s on the rugged coast of Maine, where local residents and wealthy philanthropists worked together to protect its unspoiled landscapes. Originally established as Sieur de Monts National Monument in 1916, it became Lafayette National Park in 1919 — the first national park east of the Mississippi River. Later, in ,1929, it was renamed Acadia National Park to honor the region’s early French heritage. The efforts of conservationists like George B. Dorr, known as the “Father of Acadia,” were crucial in preserving its mountains, forests, and rocky shores. Today, Acadia stands as a testament to the enduring value of conservation and the beauty of America’s Atlantic coast.

Masssai Mara National Park

The Maasai Mara National Park, located in southwestern Kenya, has a history deeply connected to the Maasai people, the region’s original inhabitants. For centuries, the Maasai lived in harmony with the land, allowing wildlife to roam freely across their open grasslands. In the early 20th century, British colonial authorities recognized the area’s exceptional beauty and rich biodiversity, leading to the creation of the Mara Triangle in 1948 as a protected wildlife sanctuary. Later, in 1961, the area officially became the Maasai Mara National Reserve, named to honor both the local Maasai community and the Mara River that flows through it. Today, it stands as one of Africa’s most famous wildlife destinations, known for the Great Migration, where millions of wildebeest and zebras cross the plains each year in one of the world’s most breathtaking natural events.

Kruger National Park

Kruger National Park, one of Africa’s largest and most famous game reserves, was established in 1898 by President Paul Kruger to protect the wildlife of the South African Lowveld. It officially became a national park in 1926, making it the first national park in South Africa. Over the years, the park has evolved from a small conservation area into a vast sanctuary that spans nearly 20,000 square kilometers.Today, Kruger is known for its incredible biodiversity, including the Big Five — lions, leopards, elephants, rhinos, and buffaloes.

Kakadu National Park

The history of Kakadu National Park stretches back over 65,000 years, making it one of the oldest continuously inhabited places on Earth. The Aboriginal people, particularly the Bininj and Mungguy communities, have lived on this land for thousands of generations. Their deep connection to the environment is reflected in ancient rock art, sacred sites, and traditional practices that tell the story of human life, spirituality, and survival. These artworks, some of which are over 20,000 years old, provide a window into one of the world’s richest cultural landscapes.

Banff National Park

The history of Banff National Park began in 1883, when three Canadian Pacific Railway workers discovered natural hot springs on the slopes of Sulphur Mountain in Alberta, Canada. This discovery led to debates over who owned the land, but it also inspired the idea of protecting the area for public benefit. In 1885, the Canadian government established a 10-square-mile reserve, marking the birth of Canada’s first national park and one of the earliest in the world,. This step laid the foundation for a proud legacy of nature conservation across Canada.

Chitawn National Park

The history of Chitwan National Park is deeply connected to Nepal’s conservation efforts and its rich wildlife heritage. Established in 1973, it became the country’s first national park and later was recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1984. Once used as a royal hunting ground, it transformed into a protected area to save endangered species like the one-horned rhinoceros and the Bengal tiger.
