A Brief History

In the years following its establishment, Yellowstone faced many challenges, including illegal hunting and vandalism. In 1917, the U.S. Army handed over park management to the newly created National Park Service, which improved conservation and visitor facilities. Today, Yellowstone is recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage Site, preserving its rich geological wonders and wildlife for future generations.

Before Yellowstone became a national park, the region was home to Native American tribes for thousands of years. Tribes such as the Shoshone, Crow, and Bannock lived, hunted, and traveled through the area. They respected the land’s natural power and beauty, often considering its geothermal features to be sacred. Their deep connection to the land remains an important part of Yellowstone’s history.
