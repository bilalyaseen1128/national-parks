Animals of yosemite National Park

American Black Bear

The American black bear is a frequent large mammal in Yosemite’s forests and meadows. According to the park’s wildlife-safety guidance, Yosemite supports over 400 species of vertebrates partly because of its intact, diverse habitats from forests to alpine rock. Black bears are omnivores, foraging for plants, berries, insects and sometimes carrion. They are protected in the park, and visitors are strongly advised not to approach or feed them because this disrupts their natural behaviour and can create dangerous situations. Nati

Mule Deer

The mule deer is common in Yosemite, especially visible in meadow and valley areas where they browse on leaves, twigs, grasses and herbs.Male mule deer (bucks) grow antlers annually and engage in dominance behaviour during mating season. Even though they appear tame, they are still wild animals and must be treated with respect and caution.

Sierra Nevada Bighorn Sheep

The Sierra Nevada bighorn sheep is a specially protected species in Yosemite — they inhabit the high-alpine country and steep rugged terrain.Their conservation is a success story, reflecting efforts to restore and protect wildlife in challenging environments. Their agility on sheer rock faces and their presence in the park’s highest elevations make them a fascinating subject.

Yellow-bellied Marmot

The yellow-bellied marmot inhabits the alpine and sub-alpine areas of Yosemite and is well-adapted to rocky, open habitats. Though smaller than the large mammals, marmots contribute to the ecosystem in their own way and often make for delightful sightings in high-elevation terrain.
